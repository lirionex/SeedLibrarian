# The wild valley

**Wild plants are the only source of new genes.** You cannot breed a trait that doesn't exist in your pool. When a request needs something nobody has isolated, the answer isn't in the greenhouse — it's out there.

**Trait geography is legible from common sense.** Need frost resistance? Go up. Salt tolerance? The coast. This gives the player a compass built from intuition rather than quest markers.

| Location | Trait | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| Snow line | cold hardiness | tiny, drab, flowerless |
| Exposed ridge | wind-tough stems | no scent (no insects up there) |
| Deep forest | night bloom, shade tolerance | very slow growth |
| Unlit caves | bioluminescence | fragile, needs constant damp |
| Coast | salt tolerance | coarse foliage |
| Meadow floor | starting stock | unremarkable |

**Every wild trait carries a drawback, and the drawback is what that environment actually cost the plant.** Cave flora is fragile because it never needed strength. Ridge plants dropped scent because there was nothing to attract. This makes the system feel discovered rather than designed, and lets players *predict* trade-offs before they climb.

So an expedition never hands you an answer. It hands you a problem shaped like an opportunity: carry the hardiness down the mountain and marry it into something beautiful over several generations. **That's where the game lives.**

Wild populations shift with season and weather, so the valley is never static.
