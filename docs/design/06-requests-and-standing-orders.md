# Requests and standing orders

A request system is one small step from being a quest log. Three things prevent that.

## Standing orders — the backbone

Permanent buyers who never finish, rewarding *refinement* rather than completion:

- a perfumer paying for any strong scent, scaled to intensity
- a dyer paying for saturation, any hue
- a botanical society paying for anything new to the world
- a grange paying for yield and hardiness

These mean you can always be productive without having accepted anything.

## Individual requests — two kinds

*Precise*, which teach the genetics:
> Highland Grange wants an oat that sets seed before first frost and tolerates poor soil.

*Interpretive*, which give the game its soul:
> Marta, three doors down: "My father is going blind. I want something on his windowsill he can still find."

The second kind is a design brief, not a spec. The player interprets it, delivers, and the client *reacts*. Partial success is a real outcome, judged on feel rather than a checkbox.

## Scarcity of attention

More requests are available than the player can take. Accepting costs bench space, seasons, and seed stock. Choosing is the gameplay; assignment isn't.

## Expiry

Tied to **occasions, not timers** — a wedding, a harvest fair, a funeral, the spring show. Legible, visible on the calendar, obviously immovable. Everything else simply waits, and impatience becomes flavour (the client writes again, more wryly the third time).

**Failing a request still leaves you the plant.** You lose the commission, not the work — it goes in the book. This is what lets players take risky requests without the game punishing curiosity.

_Not started. POC scope: one standing order (perfumer, pays for scent scaled to intensity) plus three hand-written requests (one precise, one interpretive, one with a deadline).
