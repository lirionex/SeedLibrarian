# Utilities and self-sufficiency

Running costs give money a permanent job in the late game, when tool purchases have dried up. The arc — paying the grid, then offsetting with a panel, then eventually cutting the line by choice — is the actual cottagecore fantasy: homesteading, not just gardening. It can never be "completed"; it's a number you push toward zero at your own pace.

**Utilities must feed back into the plants, not just the wallet.** Water is where this gets good:

- rainwater off the roof is soft
- well water is hard and mineral-rich
- water chemistry shifts soil pH, and pH shifts *expression*

Hydrangeas bloom blue in acid soil and pink in alkaline — same plant, same genes. Put that in, and "which cistern do I irrigate bed four from" becomes a colour decision. The infrastructure is part of the breeding game, not an economy minigame beside it.

**Buffers, not throughput.** A battery bank and a cistern you watch over days. No wire routing, no pipe puzzles, no belts. Placement matters (roof aspect for sun, water table depth for a well); connection is automatic. The moment it becomes a flow-rate optimisation problem, the genre has quietly changed and this game can't survive it.

**No failure state.** Run dry and the greenhouse drifts to ambient. Whatever you were holding at cave humidity is now just a room. **The consequence is botanical, not financial** — you lose the experiment, not the farm.

**Seasons generate tension for free:** winter is simultaneously the lowest solar yield and the highest heating demand. You bank surplus through summer knowing the squeeze is coming, which gives the year a shape you didn't have to design.

Asset cost: panels, a windmill, a wellhead, a cistern, water butts. Six props and some numbers.

**The real reward isn't the savings.** Once nothing costs anything to run, you can hold a bed at absurd conditions for a whole season just to see what happens. Self-sufficiency buys creative freedom.
