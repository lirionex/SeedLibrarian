# What you do between generations

The failure mode is a menu simulator. The answer is that the between-activities are **made out of the plants you already have**, not parallel systems.

## Processing

A still, a dye vat, a press, a drying loft. Output is a **continuous function of the plant's traits** — scent intensity determines oil yield, colour saturation determines dye strength. This isn't a crafting tree; it's a readout of your breeding. Better plants immediately mean better goods, and it feeds standing orders directly, so processing yesterday's harvest is always worth money and never a chore.

## The desk

Documenting a specimen should be an *activity* — sketch it, press a flower into the page, write observations — not a menu confirmation. A fully documented page sells for more, counts for the botanical society, and deepens understanding of that trait family. Optional, so it never becomes homework. The thing you do on a rainy evening.

## Bed preparation

Because expression depends on environment, soil composition, drainage, light, and shelter are real decisions. A cold-hardy cross won't reveal its hardiness in rich warm loam. Waiting time becomes skilled preparation, and greenhouse layout becomes a puzzle rather than a grid.

## Building

Extensions buy **capability**, not just decoration: more bench space, a controlled greenhouse, a cold frame for out-of-season work, a shopfront so clients come to you, a vault with more archive slots. Cosmetic decoration layers freely on top — and the player's own bred plants are the decor. A lamp lit by glowing cave flowers you spent nine generations stabilising is the most satisfying object you can put in a room, because you made it.

## The shape of a day

Dawn — flowers are receptive, so pollination if you want it.
Midday — beds and the still.
Evening — the desk.
Bad weather — pushes you indoors to process and document.

Nothing is scheduled. The world just keeps suggesting.
